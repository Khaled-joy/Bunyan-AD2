Bot Workshop · ورشة الروبوت

Primary: Practical (digital & teamwork) · Secondary: Cognitive (creative)

Block-based programming on a grid. Drag blocks, hit Run, watch the bot move.

L1 — First Steps. 5 puzzles, blocks limited to forward / turn / pick up. Grid is 4×4. Solution length isn't scored — just reaching the goal.
L2 — Fewer Blocks. Loops and if conditions unlock. Each puzzle has a block budget; using fewer blocks than the budget earns Gold. Teaches abstraction as a reward, not a rule.
L3 — Two Bots. Co-op. The board needs two bots doing different jobs at the same time; the program is split into two halves. Play live with a partner on a shared link, or async — you inherit a half-program someone else wrote and must make yours fit it. Custom function blocks unlock here.

Win state: goal reached, blocks used vs. budget, and (L3) whether both bots finished within the same number of ticks.

Truth Lab · مختبر الحقيقة

Primary: Core Life Skills (thinking & citizenship) · Secondary: Cognitive (analytical)

The learner is a fact-checker. Claims arrive on cards; they get sorted.

L1 — Sort It. Statements with picture support get dropped into ✅ True / ❌ False / 🤔 Not Sure. The "Not Sure" bin is scored positively when the claim really is ambiguous — the lesson is that "I don't know yet" is a valid answer.
L2 — Find the Source. Each claim now ships with 3 evidence cards (a friend's message, a news photo, an official page). Match claim → strongest evidence. Introduces: who said it, and how would they know?
L3 — Newsroom. A breaking story, 4 sources that partly contradict each other, a 90-second clock. Player publishes a verdict plus picks the two sources that justify it. Scored 40% on verdict, 60% on justification — deliberately, so guessing right badly beats out reasoning well.

Common Ground · أرض مشتركة

Primary: Social Cohesion (diversity & civic) · Secondary: Core Life Skills (citizenship)

Resource-sharing under conflicting, legitimate needs.

L1 — Fair Shares. Split 12 items among 4 characters who need different amounts (one is younger, one already has some, one is sharing with a sibling). Equal splitting is not the top score — the game surfaces the difference between equal and fair.
L2 — The Council. A neighborhood has 100 credits and 6 proposals: a ramp, a pitch, a library extension, shade trees, a bus stop, a clinic. Four stakeholder characters argue with real trade-offs. Player allocates, then sees the one-year outcome and who was left out.
L3 — Write the Rule. Player drafts a rule for a shared space using a constrained sentence-builder (subject + condition + exception). Three opposing characters raise objections; each objection must be answered by amending the rule or accepting a cost. Ends with a public vote whose threshold the player can see rising and falling live.

Spark Studio · استوديو الشرارة

Primary: Transformative (innovation & leading) · Secondary: Practical

Open-ended making. The only game with no correct answers at all.

L1 — Mash-Up. Two random objects appear (umbrella + bicycle). Assemble an invention from a parts tray, name it, give it one purpose. Everything submitted is accepted.
L2 — Under Constraint. A character has a problem and a limited parts budget. Build a solution, then pitch it in one sentence with a required structure: "For [who], this [what] so that [why]." Constraint tightens across 5 challenges.
L3 — Design Sprint. Three timed rounds — Discover (interview 3 characters, pick the real problem), Prototype (build + name), Pitch (60-second recorded or written pitch). The player directs a small team of character-helpers with different strengths, assigning each a round-task — that assignment is the leadership mechanic. Ends in a gallery where peers vote across three axes: useful / surprising / kind.

Win state: gallery placement + a "signature" tag derived from their invention history (you build for accessibility).
